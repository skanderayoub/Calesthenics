# Calisthenics Program — Adapted

**Starting point:** 4 strict pull-ups, 10 dips, 66 kg / 174 cm, age 29
**Goal:** muscle + aesthetics, plus handstand / L-sit / eventually muscle-up
**Equipment:** pull-up + dip station at home, calisthenics park
**Frequency:** 4 sessions/week, ~45 minutes each

There is no week count in this program. You move on when you hit the gates in sections 6 and 7, however long that takes. See section 13 for what changed from the first draft and why.

---

## 0. Get these two things first

| Item | Cost | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Resistance band set (loop bands) | ~€20 | Pull-up assistance, face pulls, curls, rows |
| Sturdy backpack | free | Weighted dips and pull-ups later — this is how you keep progressing past bodyweight |

Bodyweight-only stops working eventually. The backpack is what prevents that.

### No rings needed

Every exercise in this program is done on a bar, dip station, floor, or band. Nothing here requires rings.

If you ever want them later (~€30), they mainly add three things — and each has a bar alternative you're already doing:

| Ring exercise | What it gives you | Bar/floor alternative in this program |
|---|---|---|
| Ring rows | Rowing volume, shoulder-friendly | Inverted rows on a park bar |
| Ring dips | Extra stability demand, deeper range | Straight-bar dips (Phase 3), or weighted parallel-bar dips |
| Ring push-ups / flies | Chest stretch under load | Deficit push-ups — hands on two low, stable objects so your chest drops below hand level |

Rings are a nice-to-have, not a requirement. Don't wait on them to start.

## 1. The progression rule

This is what the Reddit program was missing, and it matters more than the exercise selection.

**Double progression.** Every exercise has a set count and a rep range.

1. Stay at the same variation until you hit the **top of the rep range on every set**, two sessions in a row.
2. Then progress: add load (backpack) if possible, otherwise move to the harder variation.
3. When you progress, you drop back to the bottom of the rep range. That's normal.

**One deliberate exception:** Phase 1 pull-ups. See section 5.

**Leave 1–2 reps in the tank** on most sets. Going to total failure every set on 4 days/week will bury your recovery. Last set of an exercise can go close to failure.

**Never advance phases by calendar.** Advance when you hit the criteria in sections 6 and 7.

**Log every session.** If you don't log it, you can't progress it.

---

## 2. How the sessions are built

### Supersets are what make 45 minutes work

Exercises marked with the same letter in the **Pair** column are **alternated**, not done straight through:

```
Pull-up set  →  1:15  →  Dip set  →  1:15  →  Pull-up set  →  …
```

The rest shown is the *interval between exercises*, not your recovery. Because pulling and pushing don't compete, each movement still gets roughly **double** the shown rest before you repeat it — a 1:15 alternation gives every pull-up set about 2 minutes 45 seconds of actual recovery, which is more than a straight 2–3 minutes would have been at half the wall-clock cost.

Done straight through, Upper A takes about 55 minutes. Paired, it takes 44. **This is the only reason the session fits, so don't unpair it.**

### Rest still matters

Do not cut the interval to save time. Short rests are the main reason people "get tired quickly" and then stall — you end up limited by fatigue rather than strength, which is the wrong stimulus for what you want. The pairing already bought you the time; spending it again is how the program stops working.

### Order

Skill work goes **first**, right after the warm-up, when you're fresh. Never at the end.

### The week

| Session | Typical day |
|---|---|
| Upper A — pull emphasis | Mon |
| Lower 1 — squat emphasis | Tue |
| — | Wed off |
| Upper B — push emphasis | Thu |
| Lower 2 — hinge emphasis | Fri |
| — | Sat / Sun off. Optional 10 min handstand practice — it's skill, not training |

The **order** is fixed. The **weekdays are not** — see section 9.

The two lower days are deliberately different: Lower 1 is knee-dominant (split squats, step-ups, pistols), Lower 2 is hip-dominant (Romanian deadlifts, Nordics). Doing the same leg session twice a week trains the same thing twice and leaves the hamstrings and glutes short.

## 3. Warm-up (same every session, ~8 min)

1. 2 min easy cardio — jog in place, jumping jacks, skipping
2. Arm circles ×10 each direction
3. Band or towel shoulder dislocates ×10
4. **Wrists** — palms-down rocks ×10 · palms-up rocks ×10 · fist circles ×10
5. Scapular pull-ups ×8 · scapular push-ups ×8
6. 10 bodyweight squats · 10 hip hinges · world's greatest stretch ×5/side
7. One light set of the first exercise

Wrists are step 4 for a reason: wrist pain is the single most common thing that stops handstand progress, and it is almost entirely preventable.

### Once the backpack is on

Don't go from the warm-up straight into a working set of weighted pull-ups or dips. Do **one set at bodyweight, then one at about half your working load**, then start. It costs three minutes and it's the difference between elbows that tolerate loading and elbows that don't.

## 5. Why 6 × 2 pull-ups, and why they break the rule

At a 4-rep max, sets of 5–8 mean you fail on set two and grind ugly reps for the rest. Six clean doubles gives you 12 quality reps instead of 8 sloppy ones, with far less fatigue. Volume drives progress here, not proximity to failure.

**Phase 1 pull-ups use a weekly ladder, not double progression.** Add one rep to one set each week:

```
2,2,2,2,2,2  →  3,2,2,2,2,2  →  3,3,2,2,2,2  →  …  →  3,3,3,3,3,3
```

When you reach 6 × 3, retest your max. You'll be surprised. From Phase 2 onward, pull-ups follow the normal rule in section 1 like everything else.

This is the only exercise in the program with its own rule. The app will still flag 6 × 3 as a progression — that flag is correct, it just means "time to retest".

## 8. The L-sit is a flexibility problem, not a strength one

Most people grind the tuck L-sit for months and never straighten the legs, because they're training the wrong thing. What actually gates it is **hip flexor compression** and **hamstring length** — the ability to pull your legs up, not to hold your body down.

That's what the seated pike leg lifts in Lower 2 are for. Sit with legs straight, hands flat by your hips, and lift your heels off the floor without leaning back. If they barely move, that's your L-sit answer, and no amount of tuck holds will fix it.

## 9. Missed a session

**Take the sessions in order, not by weekday.** If you miss Thursday, Upper B is simply what you do next, and everything shifts by a day.

- **Never stack two sessions in one day** to catch up.
- **Never repeat the same session twice running** to get back onto the calendar. The order matters; the weekday doesn't.
- **Missed a whole week?** Repeat the previous week's loads rather than picking up where the numbers left off. You'll be back within one session.

Four sessions a week is the target. Three is fine. Two weeks of two is a signal to look at sleep and food before blaming the program.

---

## 10. Deload

Take one week at **half the sets, same reps**, when any of these is true:

- It's been **6–8 weeks** since the last one. Take it anyway, even if everything feels fine.
- Your numbers have gone **backwards three sessions running** at the same load. Two bad sessions is noise — a short night, a skipped meal, a cold bar. Three is a signal.
- **Poor sleep, achy joints and flat motivation turn up together**, whatever the numbers say. This one usually arrives before the numbers do.

Keep skill practice through the deload. You'll come back stronger — this is a feature, not lost time.

---

## 11. Retesting

Retest when you think you're near a gate, not on a schedule.

Pick a fresh day, do the full warm-up, then **one all-out set** of each: pull-ups, dips, max wall handstand hold. Do the retest **instead of** that session's first exercise, not on top of it, then carry on with the rest of the session.

Testing maxes when you're tired tells you about your fatigue, not your strength.

---

## 12. Nutrition — the actual limiting factor for you

At 66 kg with your build, training is not what's holding back muscle growth. Food is.

- **Calories:** roughly +300–400 above maintenance. Target **0.4–0.7 kg gained per month**, which is about 0.25–0.5% of bodyweight per week. Slower than that and you're leaving most of the result on the table; faster and you're mostly adding fat. If the scale hasn't moved in three weeks, eat more.
- **Protein:** ~110–130 g/day. Spread across 3–4 meals.
- **Practical:** easiest levers are whole milk, eggs, oats, nuts, olive oil, rice. Liquid calories are the friend of anyone who struggles to eat.
- **Sleep:** 7–9 hours. More impactful than any exercise selection choice in this document.

At this rate you'd add roughly 1.5–2.5 kg over three months, most of it muscle. That is what "slow" looks like when it's working.

### Progressing a band

Bands jump in big steps, so exhaust the small adjustments first: **stand wider** on the band, then **choke up** on it, then **slow the lowering** to 3 seconds. Move to a thicker band only when all three stop working.

## 13. If you're still "getting tired quickly" after 3 weeks

Two different problems, two different fixes:

- **Breathing / heart rate spikes** → conditioning. Add two 20–30 min easy walks, runs, or bike rides on off days. Won't interfere with muscle growth at this intensity.
- **Muscles burning out, arms giving up** → you're resting too little between sets. Time your rests for two weeks and see what changes.

## 14. What changed from the first draft, and why

| Change | Reason |
|---|---|
| Everything paired into supersets | The sessions took 49–55 min against a 45 min budget. Straight-through, you'd have cut rest — the one thing section 2 says not to cut |
| Rowing 6 → 8 sets/week, face pulls 3 → 6 | Against 17 sets of pressing, 6 sets of rowing was well short. Face pulls now appear on both upper days |
| Lower split into squat-dominant and hinge-dominant | Both leg days were identical, and nothing in the program was a loaded hip hinge. Single-leg RDLs fix that |
| Side plank added | All the core work was flexion. Nothing resisted rotation |
| Seated pike leg lifts added | See section 8 |
| Wrist prep added to the warm-up | The most common reason handstand work stalls |
| Ramping sets before loaded work | Going cold into weighted pull-ups is how elbows get irritated |
| Deload trigger 2 → 3 sessions, plus a calendar trigger and fatigue markers | Two bad sessions is noise. And the best deload is the one you take before you need it |
| Bodyweight target 0.2–0.3 → 0.4–0.7 kg/month | The old rate was an advanced trainee's. It would have given you ~1 kg in three months while the document itself called food the limiting factor |
| Diamond push-ups dropped from Phase 1 | Most redundant exercise in the session — ten sets of dips a week already cover triceps. Archer push-ups come in at Phase 2 |
| Calf raises capped at 15 reps | 20 reps × 2 legs × 3 sets is four minutes of calf work in a 45 min session |
| Week numbers removed | The Phase 3 gate is 10 strict pull-ups from a start of 4. That is not a 12-week job, and a calendar that says otherwise only makes you feel behind |
| Missed-session and retest sections added | Neither was covered |
